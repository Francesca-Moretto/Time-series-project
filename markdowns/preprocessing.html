1. Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) Test

This test checks for the presence of a unit root (non-stationarity).

  • \(H_0\) (Null Hypothesis): The series is not stationary (it has a unit root).

  • \(H_1\) (Alternative Hypothesis): The series is stationary.

  • Result: The test statistic value (\(t\)) is -3.5248. This is more negative than the 1% critical value (-3.43).

  • Conclusion: Reject \(H_0\). According to this test, the series is considered stationary (with a 99% confidence level).

2. KPSS Test

This test works with the opposite hypotheses compared to the ADF test.

  • \(H_0\) (Null Hypothesis): The series is stationary (level stationarity).

  • \(H_1\) (Alternative Hypothesis): The series is not stationary.

  • Result: The p-value is 0.01 (indicating it is smaller than the printed value).

  • Conclusion: Since the p-value is lower than the standard threshold of 0.05 (5%), reject \(H_0\). According to this test, the series is not stationary.

1. Augmented Dickey-Fuller (ADF) Test

  • \(H_0\) (Null Hypothesis): The series is not stationary (has a unit root).

  • \(H_1\) (Alternative Hypothesis): The series is stationary.

  • Result: The test statistic is -19.6373. This is extremely negative and far below the 1% critical value (-3.43).

  • Conclusion: Strongly reject \(H_0\). The series is definitely stationary.

2. KPSS Test

  • \(H_0\) (Null Hypothesis): The series is stationary.

  • \(H_1\) (Alternative Hypothesis): The series is not stationary.

  • Result: The p-value is 0.1 (with the warning “p-value greater than printed p-value,” implying the actual probability is even higher, likely > 0.10).

  • Conclusion: Since the p-value is greater than 0.05, fail to reject \(H_0\). The evidence supports the conclusion that the series is stationary.

1. General Interpretation

The most striking feature of both tables is that Lag 1 is statistically insignificant (approx 0.03), while Lag 2 is significant (approx -0.17).

  • Absence of Short-Term Memory: The fact that Lag 1 is near zero in both ACF and PACF means the series does not depend on the immediately preceding month.

  • Lag 2 Dependency: The significant negative spike at Lag 2 in both functions suggests a specific 2-period relationship.

Because the ACF and PACF look somewhat similar (both have a spike at Lag 2 and are otherwise quiet), it indicates a process that can be modeled effectively by either pure AR or pure MA terms, provided they reach the second lag.

2. Best Possible Models (Candidates)

Based on the standard identification rules (Box-Jenkins methodology), here are the top candidates:

Candidate A: ARIMA(0, 1, 2)

  • Why? This relies on the ACF interpretation. The ACF shows significant activity up to Lag 2 and then largely cuts off (ignoring the minor Lag 5 noise). This suggests a Moving Average (MA) process of order \(q=2\).

  • Note: Since Lag 1 is insignificant, the model will likely estimate a very small coefficient for the first MA term (\(ma1 \approx 0\)) and a significant one for the second (\(ma2 \approx -0.17\)).

Candidate B: ARIMA(2, 1, 0)

  • Why? This relies on the PACF interpretation. The PACF shows significant activity at Lag 2 and then mostly cuts off (again, barring the Lag 5 spike). This suggests an AutoRegressive (AR) process of order \(p=2\).

  • Note: Similar to the model above, this would likely result in a model where the \(ar1\) coefficient is near zero, but \(ar2\) is significant.

# --- 3 MODELLI ---

# 1. ARIMA(2,1,0) - "La scelta del PACF"
# Include 2 termini autoregressivi per catturare quel picco al lag 2.
model_ar2 <- Arima(train$log_UMCSENT, order = c(2, 1, 0))

# 2. ARIMA(0,1,2) - "La scelta dell'ACF"
# Se leggiamo l'ACF come "significativo ai primi lag e poi zero", proviamo un MA(2).
# Questo modello assume che gli shock (errori) passati influenzino il presente.
model_ma2 <- Arima(train$log_UMCSENT, order = c(0, 1, 2))

# 3. ARIMA(1,1,1) - "Il compromesso"
# Spesso batte modelli più complessi perché è più semplice.
model_mixed <- Arima(train$log_UMCSENT, order = c(1, 1, 1))

# --- CONFRONTO ---

comparison <- data.frame(
  Model = c("ARIMA(2,1,0)", "ARIMA(0,1,2)", "ARIMA(1,1,1)"),
  AIC = c(model_ar2$aic, model_ma2$aic, model_mixed$aic),
  BIC = c(model_ar2$bic, model_ma2$bic, model_mixed$bic),
  # Aggiungiamo l'errore quadratico medio (RMSE) sul training set
  RMSE = c(accuracy(model_ar2)[2], accuracy(model_ma2)[2], accuracy(model_mixed)[2])
)

# Ordiniamo dal migliore (AIC più basso)
comparison <- comparison[order(comparison$AIC),]
print(comparison)
